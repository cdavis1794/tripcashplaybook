Texas private-flight playbook

Make private-flight quotes comparable.

A useful request includes more than city pairs. Organize the operational details before comparing aircraft and prices.

Affiliate disclosure: A referral may earn a commission. Operators, aircraft, availability, and prices must be verified for the specific trip.

Start with the mission

Record the preferred departure date and time, acceptable flexibility, passenger count, passenger mobility needs, baggage type, pets, and the complete destination. Golf clubs, skis, instruments, and oversized bags can change which aircraft are practical.

Compare airports—not only cities

Austin, Dallas–Fort Worth, and Houston each have multiple airports available to general aviation. A nearby airport can reduce ground time, while another may offer better aircraft positioning or operating conditions. Ask which airport is quoted and compare the ground journey on both ends.

Send the same brief to every quote source

  • Exact passenger count, including children
  • Preferred and acceptable departure windows
  • One-way, round trip, or multi-city schedule
  • Number, dimensions, and type of bags
  • Pet, accessibility, catering, or cabin requests
  • Preferred airports and acceptable alternatives
  • Ground-transportation needs

If the briefs differ, the prices may represent different missions and cannot be compared fairly.

Empty-leg offers require flexibility

An empty leg can be useful when its existing route and schedule match your trip. It should not be treated as a guaranteed substitute for an on-demand charter. Confirm what happens if the underlying aircraft schedule changes.

Consumer check: Verify who operates the aircraft and review the contract, cancellation terms, and complete price before paying.
