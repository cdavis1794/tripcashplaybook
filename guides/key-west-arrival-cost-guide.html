Key West arrival playbook

Fly to EYW or arrive through South Florida?

Compare the full route—not just the airfare—before choosing how to reach the island.

The three-part comparison

Build each option from your front door to your Key West lodging: airfare, bags, airport wait, ground transportation, rental car, fuel, tolls, parking, and the value of your time. A lower fare into Miami or Fort Lauderdale can stop being cheaper once the final leg is included.

Option 1: Fly directly to Key West

Direct arrival reduces the road segment and may simplify a short stay. Check schedule choices, baggage allowances, and the cost of the complete itinerary. Key West International Airport lists taxis, limousines, rental cars, ride shares, and permitted ground-transportation providers.

Option 2: Fly to South Florida and drive

This can work when the flight schedule is substantially better, the drive is part of the experience, or several travelers can share a vehicle. Add rental-car taxes, fuel, potential one-way charges, parking, and enough daylight and energy for the drive. Do not schedule a tight return connection after driving off the Keys.

Option 3: Combine Miami and Key West

A two-destination trip can make the longer route worthwhile. Treat Miami nights, car pickup, and the Key West arrival day as deliberate parts of the itinerary—not hidden friction. If Miami activities are part of the plan, research them separately through MiamiBestTours.

Do you need a car after arrival?

Many visitors spend most of their time in compact parts of Key West. Before keeping a rental car for the entire stay, compare lodging parking, planned trips outside the center, and alternative transportation. The arrival vehicle and the island vehicle do not have to be the same decision.
